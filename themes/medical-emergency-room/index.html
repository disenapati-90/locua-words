Medical Emergency Room vocabulary

Words you meet in a “Medical Emergency Room” story, each with a clear meaning and an example sentence. In the Locua app these are read in story order so the context helps you remember them.

triage medium

to sort patients based on urgency of their condition.

“The nurse had to triage six patients within minutes of the accident.”

Synonyms: prioritize, sort by severity   Opposites: neglect, ignore

stabilize easy

to make something steady or unlikely to change suddenly.

“The team worked quickly to stabilize the patient before surgery.”

Synonyms: steady, secure   Opposites: destabilize, worsen

resuscitate medium

to revive someone from unconsciousness or apparent death.

“Doctors worked for ten minutes to resuscitate the collapsed patient.”

Synonyms: revive, bring back   Opposites: let expire, abandon

acute easy

severe and sudden in onset.

“The scan revealed an acute injury requiring immediate attention.”

Synonyms: severe, sharp   Opposites: chronic, mild

prognosis medium

a forecast of the likely course of a disease or injury.

“The doctor gave a cautiously optimistic prognosis to the family.”

Synonyms: outlook, forecast   Opposites: uncertainty, blank prediction

palliative hard

treatment intended to relieve pain without addressing the underlying cause.

“When a cure wasn't possible, the team shifted to palliative care.”

Synonyms: comforting, relieving   Opposites: curative, corrective

contraindicated hard

not advisable due to a specific condition or combination of factors.

“The medication was contraindicated given her allergy history.”

Synonyms: inadvisable, unsuitable   Opposites: recommended, advisable

asymptomatic hard

showing no symptoms of a disease despite having it.

“The infection was asymptomatic, which delayed his diagnosis by weeks.”

Synonyms: symptom-free, silent   Opposites: symptomatic, visibly ill

vital signs easy

essential measurements of body function (pulse, temperature, breathing, blood pressure).

“Her vital signs were monitored continuously throughout the night.”

Synonyms: life signs, biometrics   Opposites:

malingering hard

exaggerating or faking illness to avoid duty or gain benefit.

“The physician suspected malingering rather than a genuine injury.”

Synonyms: faking, feigning   Opposites: genuine suffering, honesty
