Diplomatic Negotiation vocabulary

Words you meet in a “Diplomatic Negotiation” story, each with a clear meaning and an example sentence. In the Locua app these are read in story order so the context helps you remember them.

bilateral medium

involving two parties, especially two countries.

“The two nations signed a bilateral trade agreement after months of talks.”

Synonyms: two-sided, mutual   Opposites: unilateral, one-sided

concession medium

something given up or granted, especially to reach agreement.

“Neither side wanted to be the first to offer a concession.”

Synonyms: compromise, trade-off   Opposites: demand, refusal

ceasefire easy

a temporary suspension of fighting by mutual agreement.

“The ceasefire held for exactly eleven days before talks resumed.”

Synonyms: truce, armistice   Opposites: escalation, renewed conflict

sanctions medium

penalties imposed by one country on another to enforce compliance.

“New sanctions were imposed within days of the failed negotiations.”

Synonyms: penalties, restrictions   Opposites: incentives, concessions

envoy medium

a diplomatic messenger or representative.

“A special envoy was dispatched to mediate the escalating dispute.”

Synonyms: diplomat, representative   Opposites:

treaty easy

a formally concluded agreement between states.

“The treaty took nearly a decade to draft and ratify.”

Synonyms: accord, pact   Opposites:

sovereignty hard

the authority of a state to govern itself.

“The dispute ultimately came down to a question of sovereignty over the region.”

Synonyms: self-governance, independence   Opposites: subjugation, dependency

appeasement hard

the policy of satisfying demands to avoid conflict, often at a cost.

“Critics accused the delegation of appeasement rather than genuine diplomacy.”

Synonyms: conciliation, pacification   Opposites: confrontation, resistance

impasse medium

a situation with no apparent solution; a deadlock.

“Talks reached an impasse just hours before the deadline.”

Synonyms: deadlock, stalemate   Opposites: breakthrough, resolution

ratify medium

to formally approve or confirm an agreement.

“Parliament still needed to ratify the agreement before it took effect.”

Synonyms: approve, confirm   Opposites: reject, veto
