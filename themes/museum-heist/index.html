Museum Heist vocabulary

Words you meet in a “Museum Heist” story, each with a clear meaning and an example sentence. In the Locua app these are read in story order so the context helps you remember them.

heist easy

a robbery, especially a daring or skillfully executed one.

“The heist was executed in under six minutes, with no alarms triggered.”

Synonyms: robbery, theft   Opposites: recovery, restitution

surveillance easy

close observation, especially of a suspected person.

“Detectives reviewed hours of surveillance footage looking for the culprit.”

Synonyms: monitoring, observation   Opposites: neglect, inattention

forgery medium

the act of producing a fake copy of something to deceive.

“Experts later confirmed the painting left behind was an elaborate forgery.”

Synonyms: counterfeit, fake   Opposites: authentic original, genuine article

accomplice easy

a person who helps another commit a crime.

“Police believed an accomplice on the inside had disabled the cameras.”

Synonyms: co-conspirator, partner in crime   Opposites: informant, witness

provenance medium

the origin or history of ownership of an object.

“The painting's provenance had never been fully verified, raising suspicion for years.”

Synonyms: origin, history of ownership   Opposites:

clandestine hard

done secretly, especially something illicit.

“The clandestine meeting took place in a warehouse far from the city center.”

Synonyms: secret, covert   Opposites: open, public

fence medium

a person who deals in stolen goods.

“The thieves needed a fence willing to move stolen art quietly.”

Synonyms: dealer in stolen goods, receiver   Opposites:

incriminate medium

to make someone appear guilty of a crime.

“The fingerprint on the frame was enough to incriminate the guard.”

Synonyms: implicate, inculpate   Opposites: exonerate, clear

elude medium

to escape or avoid someone or something in a skillful way.

“For years, the mastermind managed to elude international investigators.”

Synonyms: evade, escape   Opposites: confront, get caught

ransom easy

a sum demanded for the release of a captive or stolen item.

“The thieves demanded a ransom in exchange for the painting's return.”
