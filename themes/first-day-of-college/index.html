First Day of College vocabulary

Words you meet in a “First Day of College” story, each with a clear meaning and an example sentence. In the Locua app these are read in story order so the context helps you remember them.

orientation easy

an introductory event to familiarize newcomers with a place or system.

“Orientation week left her both exhausted and quietly excited for what came next.”

Synonyms: induction, introduction   Opposites:

syllabus easy

an outline of topics to be covered in a course.

“The syllabus for organic chemistry alone looked more intense than his entire senior year.”

Synonyms: course outline, curriculum plan   Opposites:

prerequisite medium

a requirement that must be completed before something else.

“She hadn't realized statistics was a prerequisite for the course she wanted.”

Synonyms: requirement, precondition   Opposites:

matriculate hard

to enroll formally as a student at a college or university.

“He would officially matriculate the following Monday, after years of preparation.”

Synonyms: enroll, register   Opposites: withdraw, drop out

cohort medium

a group of people with a shared characteristic, especially in education.

“Her cohort of two hundred students would take almost every class together.”

Synonyms: class group, peer group   Opposites:

extracurricular easy

an activity outside the regular academic curriculum.

“She signed up for three extracurricular clubs before classes had even started.”

Synonyms: outside activity, club activity   Opposites: curricular, coursework

autonomy medium

the freedom to make one's own decisions independently.

“The sudden autonomy of managing his own schedule was thrilling and terrifying at once.”

Synonyms: independence, self-governance   Opposites: dependence, restriction

assimilate medium

to adjust and become fully part of a new environment or group.

“It took her a full semester to assimilate into the new social rhythm.”

Synonyms: adapt, integrate   Opposites: alienate, isolate

procrastinate easy

to delay or postpone action.

“Without anyone checking in on him, it was far too easy to procrastinate.”

Synonyms: delay, postpone   Opposites: act promptly, expedite

homesick easy

experiencing a longing for one's home while away.

“By the second week, she was more homesick than she'd expected to be.”

Synonyms: longing for home, nostalgic   Opposites: settled, content away from home
