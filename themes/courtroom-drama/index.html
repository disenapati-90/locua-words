Courtroom Drama vocabulary

Words you meet in a “Courtroom Drama” story, each with a clear meaning and an example sentence. In the Locua app these are read in story order so the context helps you remember them.

perjury hard

the offense of lying while under oath in a court of law.

“The witness was charged with perjury after admitting she had lied under oath.”

Synonyms: false testimony, lying under oath   Opposites: honesty, truthfulness

culpable hard

deserving blame; responsible for wrongdoing.

“The prosecutor argued that the defendant was fully culpable for the fraud.”

Synonyms: blameworthy, guilty   Opposites: innocent, blameless

exculpatory hard

tending to clear someone of blame or fault.

“New exculpatory evidence emerged just before the verdict was announced.”

Synonyms: vindicating, clearing   Opposites: incriminating, damning

indict medium

to formally charge someone with a crime.

“The grand jury voted to indict the company's former CEO.”

Synonyms: charge, accuse   Opposites: exonerate, acquit

litigious hard

prone to taking legal disputes to court.

“Their notoriously litigious neighbor had sued three people on the street already.”

Synonyms: contentious, quarrelsome   Opposites: conciliatory, agreeable

equivocate hard

to use ambiguous language to avoid committing to a clear statement.

“Under cross-examination, the witness began to equivocate rather than answer directly.”

Synonyms: hedge, dodge   Opposites: clarify, affirm

obfuscate hard

to deliberately make something unclear or hard to understand.

“The defense was accused of trying to obfuscate the timeline of events.”

Synonyms: confuse, muddle   Opposites: clarify, illuminate

remonstrate hard

to protest or argue forcefully in objection to something.

“The lawyer stood to remonstrate against the judge's sudden ruling.”

Synonyms: protest, object   Opposites: comply, acquiesce

vindicate hard

to clear someone of blame after they've been suspected or accused.

“The final ruling would either vindicate her or end her career.”

Synonyms: exonerate, absolve   Opposites: incriminate, condemn

acquiesce hard

to accept or comply with something without protest.

“Rather than fight the settlement further, the company chose to acquiesce.”

Synonyms: comply, yield   Opposites: resist, object
