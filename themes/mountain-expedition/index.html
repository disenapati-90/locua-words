Mountain Expedition vocabulary

Words you meet in a “Mountain Expedition” story, each with a clear meaning and an example sentence. In the Locua app these are read in story order so the context helps you remember them.

altitude easy

the height of an object or point above sea level.

“At this altitude, even simple tasks left them gasping for air.”

Synonyms: elevation, height   Opposites:

acclimatize medium

to adjust physically to a new climate or altitude.

“The team spent two extra days at base camp to acclimatize properly.”

Synonyms: adapt, adjust   Opposites:

crevasse medium

a deep, open crack in a glacier or ice sheet.

“A hidden crevasse nearly ended the expedition on day two.”

Synonyms: glacial crack, fissure   Opposites:

perilous medium

full of danger or risk.

“The final ridge was the most perilous stretch of the entire climb.”

Synonyms: dangerous, hazardous   Opposites: safe, secure

summit easy

the highest point of a mountain.

“They reached the summit just as the sun broke over the horizon.”

Synonyms: peak, top   Opposites: base, foot

endurance easy

the ability to withstand prolonged physical or mental strain.

“The climb tested not just their strength but their sheer endurance.”

Synonyms: stamina, resilience   Opposites: fatigue, weakness

hypothermia hard

a dangerous drop in body temperature caused by cold exposure.

“Signs of hypothermia forced the guide to turn two climbers back early.”

Synonyms: cold exposure illness, extreme chill   Opposites:

precipice medium

a very steep cliff or rock face.

“One wrong step near the precipice would have been fatal.”

Synonyms: cliff edge, steep drop   Opposites:

relentless medium

unyielding in severity or intensity; not stopping.

“The relentless wind made every hour above camp feel like three.”

Synonyms: unrelenting, persistent   Opposites: intermittent, mild

triumphant easy

feeling or expressing great success or victory.

“Exhausted but triumphant, the team radioed base camp from the summit.”

Synonyms: victorious, exultant   Opposites: defeated, despondent
