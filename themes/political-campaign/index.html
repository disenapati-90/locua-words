Political Campaign vocabulary

Words you meet in a “Political Campaign” story, each with a clear meaning and an example sentence. In the Locua app these are read in story order so the context helps you remember them.

constituency easy

a body of voters in a specific area who elect a representative.

“The candidate spent the weekend visiting every town in her constituency.”

Synonyms: electorate, voter base   Opposites:

canvass medium

to go through a district seeking votes or opinions.

“Volunteers were sent out to canvass the neighborhood before election day.”

Synonyms: poll, survey   Opposites: ignore, avoid contact

incumbent medium

the current holder of an office or position.

“The incumbent was widely expected to win a second term easily.”

Synonyms: officeholder, sitting official   Opposites: challenger, outsider

partisan medium

strongly biased in support of a particular party or cause.

“The debate quickly turned partisan, with little room for compromise.”

Synonyms: biased, one-sided   Opposites: neutral, impartial

gerrymander hard

to manipulate electoral district boundaries for political advantage.

“Critics accused the state legislature of trying to gerrymander the new district map.”

Synonyms: manipulate districts, rig boundaries   Opposites: redistrict fairly, reapportion equitably

demagogue hard

a leader who gains popularity by appealing to emotion and prejudice.

“Historians later described him as a demagogue who thrived on public fear.”

Synonyms: rabble-rouser, agitator   Opposites: statesman, diplomat

mandate medium

authority granted by voters to carry out a policy or course of action.

“With such a wide margin, she claimed a clear mandate to govern.”

Synonyms: authorization, sanction   Opposites: rejection, opposition

filibuster hard

a prolonged speech used to obstruct legislative action.

“The senator's filibuster delayed the vote for over twelve hours.”

Synonyms: obstruction, stalling tactic   Opposites: expedite, facilitate

coalition easy

a temporary alliance of groups working together for a common purpose.

“No single party held a majority, forcing a fragile coalition government.”

Synonyms: alliance, bloc   Opposites: division, faction

electorate easy

all the people in a country or area entitled to vote.

“The electorate turned out in record numbers this election cycle.”

Synonyms: voters, constituents   Opposites:
