Startup Life vocabulary

Words you meet in a “Startup Life” story, each with a clear meaning and an example sentence. In the Locua app these are read in story order so the context helps you remember them.

bootstrap easy

to build something using only existing resources, without external help.

“With no outside funding, the founders had to bootstrap the entire product themselves.”

Synonyms: self-fund, self-start   Opposites: outsource, subsidize

pivot easy

to change strategy or direction significantly.

“After months of flat growth, the team decided to pivot to a new market entirely.”

Synonyms: shift, redirect   Opposites: persist, continue unchanged

runway medium

the amount of time a company can operate before running out of money.

“With only four months of runway left, the CEO scrambled to close a new funding round.”

Synonyms: cash reserve period, financial buffer   Opposites: insolvency, bankruptcy

scalable medium

capable of being expanded or grown without a proportional rise in cost or effort.

“Investors were drawn to the idea because it seemed genuinely scalable across markets.”

Synonyms: expandable, growth-ready   Opposites: limited, capped

attrition medium

the gradual reduction of staff through resignation or retirement.

“High attrition in the engineering team worried the board more than the missed deadlines.”

Synonyms: turnover, erosion   Opposites: retention, stability

synergy medium

the combined effect of two things being greater than their separate effects.

“The merger was pitched as pure synergy, though few employees actually saw it that way.”

Synonyms: cooperation, combined effect   Opposites: conflict, redundancy

cannibalize hard

to reduce the sales of one product by introducing another similar one.

“The new product risked cannibalizing sales of their own flagship offering.”

Synonyms: undercut, self-compete   Opposites: complement, boost

disintermediation hard

the removal of intermediaries between producers and consumers.

“By selling directly to consumers, the startup drove disintermediation across the entire industry.”

Synonyms: cutting out the middleman, direct distribution   Opposites: intermediation, channel reliance

capitulate hard

to cease resisting and accept an opponent's demands.

“Facing a hostile takeover bid, the board eventually chose to capitulate.”

Synonyms: surrender, concede   Opposites: resist, hold out

obsolescence hard

the state of being outdated or no longer used.

“The founders lived in constant fear of technological obsolescence.”

Synonyms: outdatedness, redundancy   Opposites: relevance, currency
