Volcanic Eruption Survival vocabulary

Words you meet in a “Volcanic Eruption Survival” story, each with a clear meaning and an example sentence. In the Locua app these are read in story order so the context helps you remember them.

evacuate easy

to remove people from a dangerous place to safety.

“Authorities ordered the entire village to evacuate within the hour.”

Synonyms: clear out, remove to safety   Opposites: remain, stay put

dormant medium

temporarily inactive but capable of becoming active again.

“Locals had long assumed the volcano was permanently dormant.”

Synonyms: inactive, sleeping   Opposites: active, erupting

seismic medium

relating to earthquakes or vibrations in the earth's crust.

“Seismic activity had spiked for three days before the eruption began.”

Synonyms: earthquake-related, tremor-related   Opposites:

pyroclastic hard

relating to fragments of hot rock ejected during a volcanic eruption.

“The pyroclastic flow moved faster than anyone could outrun.”

Synonyms: volcanic debris-related, ash-flow related   Opposites:

molten easy

melted, especially rock or metal made liquid by intense heat.

“Molten rock crept down the slope at a terrifying pace.”

Synonyms: liquefied, melted   Opposites: solid, hardened

devastation medium

severe and widespread destruction.

“The devastation stretched for miles beyond the original evacuation zone.”

Synonyms: destruction, ruin   Opposites: preservation, restoration

resilience medium

the capacity to recover quickly from difficulties.

“The community's resilience in rebuilding surprised even seasoned aid workers.”

Synonyms: toughness, recovery strength   Opposites: fragility, vulnerability

displacement medium

the forced removal of people from their home area.

“Displacement affected nearly ten thousand families across the region.”

Synonyms: forced relocation, uprooting   Opposites: settlement, resettlement

aftermath easy

the period or state following a destructive event.

“In the aftermath, aid convoys arrived from three neighboring countries.”

Synonyms: consequences, aftereffects   Opposites: prelude, buildup

catastrophic easy

involving sudden, widespread, and severe damage or harm.

“Engineers called the structural failure catastrophic but avoidable.”

Synonyms: disastrous, calamitous   Opposites: minor, negligible
